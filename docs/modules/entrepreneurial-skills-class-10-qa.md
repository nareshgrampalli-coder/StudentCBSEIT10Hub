## <u>Session 1: Entrepreneurship and Society</u>

### 1. What qualities are required for a successful entrepreneur?

**Answer:**

Successful entrepreneurs have the following qualities:

- They are confident and believe in themselves and their abilities.
- They keep trying new business ideas.
- They are creative and think differently.
- They do not give up when they face difficulties.
- They make decisions after considering them carefully.
- They are patient.
- They take responsibility for their actions.
- They work hard.

### 2. What do entrepreneurs do while running a business?

**Answer:**

Entrepreneurs perform the following activities:

- **Fulfill customer needs:** Identify what people want and develop a product or service to meet that demand.
- **Use local materials:** Use available materials and people to produce low-cost products.
- **Help society:** Create businesses that benefit the community, such as schools, clinics, or environmental projects.
- **Create jobs:** Purchase more materials and hire more people to work in the business.
- **Share wealth:** As businesses grow, entrepreneurs create opportunities for people to earn and maintain a comfortable life.
- **Lower product prices:** Competition among entrepreneurs may increase supply and reduce the price of products.

## <u>Session 2: Qualities and Functions of an Entrepreneur</u>

### 3. What are the qualities of an entrepreneur?

**Answer:**

The qualities of an entrepreneur are:

- **Confidence:** Belief in oneself and one’s abilities.
- **Creativity:** Ability to think of different ways to solve problems.
- **Hard work:** Working hard on every task.
- **Patience:** Understanding that problems take time to solve.
- **Responsibility:** Taking responsibility for actions and mistakes.
- **Perseverance:** Not giving up when faced with problems.
- **Innovation:** Trying new ideas.
- **Decision-making ability:** Thinking before making decisions.

## <u>Session 3: Myths about Entrepreneurship</u>

### 4. What are the misconceptions about entrepreneurship?

**Answer:**

Common misconceptions about entrepreneurship are:

- Every business idea must be unique or exceptional.
- A person needs a lot of money to start a business.
- Entrepreneurs are born, not made.
- Only a person who has a big business is an entrepreneur.

## <u>Session 4: Entrepreneurship as a Career Option</u>

### 5. What is wage employment?

**Answer:**

**Wage employment** is based on work grades and performance parameters. Its purpose is to reward employees and maintain high performance standards.

Wage employment involves less personal financial risk, while entrepreneurship requires entrepreneurs to take risks.

### 6. What is the difference between self-employment and wage employment?

**Answer:**

There are two main ways in which a person can earn a living:

- **Self-employment:** An entrepreneur starts and runs a business to meet a need through experimentation and new ideas.
- **Wage employment:** A person works for an organization and earns wages based on work grades and performance parameters.

**Stages of an entrepreneurial business**

- **Enter:** The entrepreneur starts the business.
- **Survive:** The entrepreneur competes successfully in the market.
- **Grow:** The entrepreneur expands the business after it becomes stable.

### 7. What is entrepreneurship?

**Answer:**

**Entrepreneurship** is the process of creating a new business, taking risks, introducing new ideas, and becoming self-employed.

An **entrepreneur** is a person who starts a business, takes responsibility, and uses new ideas to meet a need or provide a service.

### 8. What different ideas do entrepreneurs add?

**Answer:**

Entrepreneurs add new ideas such as:

- Variations of products
- New services
- Cost-reduction ideas
- New marketing techniques

### 9. What are the characteristics of entrepreneurs?

**Answer:**

Important characteristics of entrepreneurs include:

- Adaptability and flexibility to achieve quality and customer satisfaction
- Belief in hard work
- Ability to take risks
- Effective money management
- Knowledge of products and services and their market demand
- Effective planning and execution
- Financial literacy
