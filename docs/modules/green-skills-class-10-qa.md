## <u>Session 1: Sustainable Development</u>

### 1. What is sustainable development?

**Answer:**

**Sustainable development** means development that satisfies the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

It maintains a balance among:

- Economic growth
- Care for the environment
- Social well-being

### 2. What is the importance of sustainable development?

**Answer:**

Sustainable development:

- Enhances quality of life.
- Protects ecosystems and natural resources for future generations.
- Helps future generations avoid severe environmental problems.
- Encourages society to adopt a development strategy that protects the environment.

As population and income increase, the consumption of products and use of natural resources also increase. These resources are required for producing goods, so development must conserve resources and minimize environmental damage.

### 3. What are the major problems related to sustainable development?

**Answer:**

The three major problems are:

- **Food:**

  - Increasing land use leaves less fertile land for crops such as wheat, rice, and other grains.
  - Chemical fertilizers reduce soil nutrients and pollute the soil.

- **Water:**

  - Fresh water from rivers and ponds is used for drinking and cleaning.
  - Garbage is dumped into rivers and ponds, making them more polluted.
  - Continued pollution may leave no clean water for future use.

- **Fuel:**

  - Wood is used as fuel and for homes and furniture.
  - Cutting down trees affects the local climate.
  - It can contribute to floods, extreme heat or cold, and other severe weather affecting people.

### 4. What steps should be taken for sustainable development?

**Answer:**

Important steps include:

- Reducing excessive resource consumption and improving resource conservation.
- Recycling and reusing waste materials.
- Managing renewable resources, especially bio-resources, scientifically.
- Planting more trees.
- Adding green grassy patches and trees between concrete buildings.
- Using environmentally friendly or biodegradable materials.
- Using environmentally friendly technologies based on resource efficiency.

### 5. What are the different Sustainable Development Goals?

**Answer:**

The concept of sustainable development first appeared in the **Brundtland Report** in 1987. The report warned countries about environmental consequences, globalization, and economic growth.

The **Sustainable Development Goals**, also known as the **Global Goals**, were introduced in 2015. There are **17 goals**:

1. No Poverty
2. Zero Hunger
3. Good Health and Well-Being
4. Quality Education
5. Gender Equality
6. Clean Water and Sanitation
7. Affordable and Clean Energy
8. Decent Work and Economic Growth
9. Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure
10. Reduced Inequalities
11. Sustainable Cities and Communities
12. Responsible Consumption and Production
13. Climate Action
14. Life below Water
15. Life on Land
16. Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions
17. Partnerships for the Goals

### 6. What sustainable-development initiatives have been taken by people in India?

**Answer:**

Examples include:

- **Mr. Vasth Hedge:** When plastic was banned in Mangalore, he created a biodegradable bag that dissolves in hot water and decomposes in the natural environment.
- **Narayana Peesapath:** He created edible cutlery using grain. The spoons can be eaten and, if uneaten, decompose easily in soil.

### 7. What are sustainable processes?

**Answer:**

**Organic farming** is a sustainable process. Organic farmers:

- Do not use chemical pesticides or fertilizers.
- Use organic and natural fertilizers such as cow dung.
- Produce higher-quality, chemical-free crops.
- Preserve soil quality for future use.

## <u>Session 2: Our Role in Sustainable Development</u>

### 8. What is our role in sustainable development?

**Answer:**

Sustainable development helps find solutions to the world’s growing problems.

Countries should establish laws and guidelines to:

- Eliminate poverty and hunger
- Improve health
- Provide quality education

Important Sustainable Development Goals include:

- Quality Education
- Clean Water and Sanitation
- Affordable and Clean Energy
- Decent Work and Economic Growth
- Reduced Inequalities
- Sustainable Cities and Communities
- Responsible Consumption and Production
- Life below Water
- Life on Land
